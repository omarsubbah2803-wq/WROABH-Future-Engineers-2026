# Parallel Steering Mechanism

## Steering Concept

Our current vehicle uses a parallel steering mechanism.

The two steering wheels are connected through a mechanical linkage. When the steering actuator moves, the linkage transfers the movement to both steering wheels.

## What Is Parallel Steering?

In parallel steering, the steering wheels are designed to turn together at approximately the same steering angle.

This is different from Ackermann steering. In Ackermann steering, the inner wheel turns through a larger angle than the outer wheel during a corner.

## Why We Chose Parallel Steering

We selected parallel steering because it provides a simpler mechanical linkage using the LEGO Technic components available to our team.

The linkage provides a direct mechanical connection between the steering actuator and both steering wheels.

The simpler mechanism also makes the steering system easier to understand, assemble and adjust.

## Ackermann Comparison

Ackermann steering was considered as an alternative during the design process.

Ackermann steering uses different steering angles for the inner and outer wheels during a turn.

Our current vehicle uses parallel steering because this was the steering geometry selected for our actual mechanical design.

A technical reference comparing Ackermann and parallel steering is included in the Technical References section.

## Steering Mechanism

The steering actuator moves the steering linkage.

The linkage transfers this movement to the steering arms connected to the steering wheels.

The mechanical connection between the actuator, linkage and wheels is important because unwanted play can affect the actual steering angle.

## Mechanical Considerations

Important considerations for the steering mechanism include:

- Low unwanted mechanical play
- Good rigidity
- Secure connections
- Repeatable steering movement
- Stable steering center
