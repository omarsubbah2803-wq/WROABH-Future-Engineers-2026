# Motor Drive System

## Purpose

The motor drive system is responsible for moving the vehicle forward and backward.

The system converts electrical energy supplied to the motors into mechanical rotation and then transfers that rotation to the driving axle and wheels.

## Motors

Our vehicle uses LEGO Large Motors for propulsion.

The motors produce rotational movement that is transferred through the mechanical drivetrain.

The exact final motor arrangement is based on the physical vehicle used by our team.

## Mechanical Power Flow

The basic power flow is:

Motor
↓
Gear Train
↓
Drive Axle
↓
Drive Wheels
↓
Vehicle Motion

## Gear Train

The drivetrain uses mechanical gears to transfer rotational movement.

The size of the gears affects the relationship between motor speed, axle speed and available torque.

A reduction in output speed can increase the available torque at the output.

This creates a trade-off between speed and torque.

Higher available torque can help the vehicle maintain movement under load, while higher output speed can allow faster movement.

The final gear ratio will be documented after the physical drivetrain is verified.

## Mechanical Coupling

The drive system uses a mechanically connected drivetrain.

The propulsion system is not intended to operate as an electronic left/right differential-drive system.

This is important because the WRO Future Engineers 2026 rules require a four-wheeled vehicle with one driving axle and a steering actuator.

The rules also state that the driving motors must be connected directly or indirectly through a gearing system to the axle turning the wheels.

## Drive and Steering

The drive system and steering system have separate functions.

The drive motors provide forward or backward movement.

The steering actuator changes the direction of the steering wheels through the steering linkage.

This separation allows the vehicle to behave as a car with propulsion and steering controlled as different mechanical systems.

## Mechanical Reliability

The drivetrain needs to remain stable during operation.

Important considerations include:

- Secure gear connections
- Correct gear alignment
- Secure axle connections
- Low unwanted mechanical play
- Rigid structural support
- Reliable wheel attachment

Unwanted movement in the drivetrain can reduce the consistency of vehicle movement.
