# Motor Drive System

## Purpose

The motor drive system converts electrical energy from the vehicle battery into mechanical movement.

The propulsion system is responsible for moving the vehicle forward and backward while the steering system is responsible for changing its direction.

## Motors

Our vehicle uses LEGO Large Motors for propulsion.

The motors produce rotational motion. This rotational motion is transferred through the mechanical drivetrain to the driving axle and wheels.

## Mechanical Power Flow

The basic power flow of the drivetrain is:

Motor
↓
Gear Train
↓
Drive Axle
↓
Drive Wheels
↓
Vehicle Motion

## Gear Train

The gear train transfers rotational motion from the motors to the driving axle.

The gear sizes determine the relationship between motor speed and output speed.

A reduction gear arrangement can reduce output speed while increasing the available torque at the output.

This creates a trade-off between speed and torque.

Higher torque can help the vehicle maintain movement under load, while higher speed can reduce the time needed to complete the track.

The final gear ratio will be documented after the physical drivetrain is verified.

## Mechanical Coupling

The propulsion system is mechanically connected to the driving axle.

The current design does not use an electronic left/right differential-drive system.

This is important because the WRO Future Engineers 2026 rules require the driving motors to be connected directly or indirectly through gearing to the axle turning the wheels.

The rules also do not allow the two driving motors to be independently connected to the driving wheels as a differential-drive system.

## Drive Control

The software controls the propulsion motors while respecting the mechanical drivetrain.

The final software documentation will explain how motor commands are converted into vehicle movement.

Important parameters such as speed, acceleration and braking behavior will be documented after the final competition software is completed.

## Mechanical Reliability

The drivetrain needs to remain mechanically secure during operation.

Important considerations include:

- Gear alignment
- Secure axle connections
- Low unnecessary mechanical play
- Structural rigidity
- Reliable wheel attachment

Any mechanical play in the drivetrain can reduce the consistency of the vehicle's movement.
