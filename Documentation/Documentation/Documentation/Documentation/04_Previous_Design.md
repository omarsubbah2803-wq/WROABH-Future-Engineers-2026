# Previous Design

## Earlier Concept

During the development of our WRO Future Engineers vehicle, we considered an earlier robot design with a more differential-style approach to propulsion.

The previous concept was built around a different mechanical architecture from the vehicle we use now.

## Why We Did Not Use the Previous Design

The WRO Future Engineers 2026 rules require the vehicle to have four wheels, one driving axle and one steering actuator.

The rules also prohibit a differential-wheeled base and the use of an electronic differential with one motor per side.

Because of these requirements, we decided not to continue with the previous architecture.

## Current Design

Our current vehicle uses:

- Four wheels
- A mechanically coupled drive system
- A dedicated steering mechanism
- Parallel steering
- LEGO Mindstorms EV3
- Pixy2 camera
- Ultrasonic sensor

## Main Design Change

The main change was moving from the previous propulsion concept to a mechanically coupled drivetrain with dedicated steering.

This gives the vehicle a clear separation between:

- Propulsion
- Steering
- Sensing
- Control

## Why the Current Design Was Selected

The current design was selected because it fits the required vehicle architecture more closely and provides a dedicated mechanical steering system.

Parallel steering was also selected because its linkage is straightforward to implement and adjust using the LEGO Technic components available to our team.

## Engineering Trade-Off

The previous concept offered a different approach to controlling vehicle movement.

The current concept requires a dedicated steering mechanism, but it also provides a mechanically defined steering system and a separate drivetrain.

The final comparison should be based on the actual mechanical behavior and physical testing of both designs.

## Development

The design change is part of our engineering development process.
